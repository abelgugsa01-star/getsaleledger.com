Why this has to happen every month

A trust (escrow) account holds money that is not yours: buyer payments waiting to be settled and consignor proceeds waiting to be paid out. Your commission is the only part that ever belongs to the business. Most state auction boards do not ask you to file anything periodically — they reserve the right to examine the account whenever they choose, and several spell out what the records must look like:

  • Wisconsin (SPS 125.12): reconcile the trust account in writing each month and keep a check register with date, payee, number and amount.
  • Texas (16 TAC §67.70(b)): itemized records of gross proceeds, expenses, every purchaser and every selling price, kept at least two years.
  • Ohio (ORC 4707.024): detailed records of deposits, withdrawals and interest in the client trust account.
  • Illinois (68 Ill. Admin. Code Pt. 1440): a chronological journal of escrow receipts and disbursements with a running balance after each entry.

Whatever your state says, the practical test is the same: on any day, can you show that the money in the bank equals what you owe consignors, and explain every dollar of the difference? Doing that once a month, while the deposits and checks are fresh, is far cheaper than doing it under examination.

This guide explains a bookkeeping routine. It is not legal or accounting advice; your state rules and your CPA govern. Check the current text of your board's rule before relying on any citation above.

The three-way check

A bank reconciliation proves your checkbook matches the bank. A trust reconciliation has to prove one more thing: that the money in the account is fully explained by what you owe. So three figures are compared, not two.

1 · Bank adjusted balance
Statement ending balance + deposits in transit − outstanding checks. What the bank will show once everything clears.
2 · Trust control balance
Opening balance + every receipt − every disbursement, from your own records for the period. What your books say is in trust.
3 · Consignor subledger total
The sum of what is still owed to each consignor (and any buyer credits). Who the money belongs to.

1 = 2 = 3  →  IN BALANCE.   Anything else → OUT OF BALANCE, with a reason for every dollar.

If 1 ≠ 2, something hit the bank that is not in your records, or vice-versa (a fee, a wire, a deposit you never entered). If 2 ≠ 3, your records are internally inconsistent — a consignor was over- or under-paid, or money is sitting in the account with no owner. Both need a named explanation, not a plug figure.

The monthly routine

  1. Fix the period. First to last day of the month, or the settlement window of one auction if that is how your board thinks. Use the same convention every time.
  2. Get the opening balance. Last month's reconciled ending bank balance. Not the current balance in online banking — the figure you proved last month.
  3. Pull three records for the period. (a) The settlement export from your auction software — Auction Flex Consignor Summary, Totals by Consignment Order, or Totals by Item; (b) the QuickBooks / IIF export of consignor payouts with dates and check numbers; (c) the trust account bank statement as CSV, Excel or OFX/QFX. Include every auction that had activity in the period — an auction you leave out shows up as unexplained deposits and checks.
  4. List receipts. Buyer payments, deposits and any interest, by date and amount. Tie each to an auction.
  5. List disbursements. Consignor payouts (check number, payee, amount), refunds to buyers, your commission transfer to operating. Tie each consignor payout to the settlement it pays.
  6. Compute the three balances and compare them.
  7. Explain every difference by name. Timing items (deposits in transit, outstanding checks) are fine as long as they clear next month. Everything else is an exception — see the list below — and gets a decision: correct the books, recover the money, or move it.
  8. Write it down. The report with the three figures, the exception list with what you decided, and the check register. Sign and date it. File it with the month's statements.
  9. Carry forward. This month's reconciled ending balance becomes next month's opening balance.
Match consignors by the exact name your auction software and your accounting file use. A consignor spelled two ways is the most common reason a payout "disappears" from the reconciliation. Fix the spelling in the source rather than guessing at the match.

The exceptions you will actually see

Almost every out-of-balance month comes down to one of these. Each has a name, a usual cause, and a right response.

ExceptionWhat it looks likeWhat to do
Disbursement mismatchA consignor check cleared for $27,700.00; the settlement says $27,200.00.Overpayment — recover it or document the correction. Underpayments are owed immediately.
Unmatched receiptA $20,000.00 deposit with no auction or buyer behind it.Find the source before it sits in trust. Money with no owner is the first thing an examiner asks about.
Unmatched disbursementA $2,950.00 wire to the operating account with no settlement authorising it.The classic commingling finding. Return it or document the commission it represents, with the settlement that supports it.
Unauthorized deductionA consignor was paid less than the settlement, with no fee or adjustment on record.Pay the difference or record the agreed deduction on the settlement.
Bank fee taken from trustA $95.00 monthly service charge.Reimburse trust from operating, or record it and reduce the fee at the bank. Fees must not come out of consignor money.
Stale fundsA consignor balance still unpaid months after settlement; an uncashed check.Re-issue or escheat per your state's unclaimed-property rules. Do not let it net against other consignors.
Timing itemsDeposits in transit, outstanding checks at month-end.Normal — list them. If the same item is still outstanding next month, it stops being a timing item.
Missing periodAn auction's settlements aren't in this month's records.Add the settlement export. Its deposits and checks are otherwise "unexplained".

The rule that keeps you out of trouble: flag, don't guess. A plug entry that makes the numbers agree is worse than an honest exception, because it hides the thing an examiner is looking for.

What to keep, and for how long

  • The monthly reconciliation report — the three balances, the status, every exception and its resolution, signed and dated.
  • The check register — date, payee, check number, amount, for every trust disbursement (the exact list Wisconsin SPS 125.12 asks for).
  • The source records — settlement exports, bank statements, deposit slips, the QuickBooks export — so the report can be reproduced from evidence, not memory.
  • A history — one report per period, in order, so you can show an examiner every month you reconciled and not just the last one.

Retention: at least two years is the floor in Texas; several states say three or five. Keep the whole set for the longest period any rule that applies to you names, and keep it where a consignor dispute can find it.

The month-end checklist

Print this page (the header and footer drop out) and tick it off each month.

  • Period fixed; opening balance = last month's reconciled ending balance.
  • Settlement export for every auction with activity this period.
  • QuickBooks / IIF payout export with check numbers and dates.
  • Trust account bank statement for the full period.
  • Receipts listed and tied to an auction.
  • Disbursements listed and tied to a settlement.
  • Bank adjusted balance, trust control balance and consignor subledger total computed.
  • Status recorded: IN BALANCE or OUT OF BALANCE.
  • Every exception named, with a decision and a date.
  • Check register attached.
  • Report signed, dated, filed with the statements.
  • Ending balance carried forward to next month.

Questions people ask

Is a bank reconciliation in QuickBooks enough?

It proves your books match the bank (balances 1 and 2). It does not prove that what is in the bank is fully owed to identifiable consignors (balance 3). That third comparison is what makes it a trust reconciliation.

Can my commission stay in the trust account?

Earned commission should move to operating promptly and be documented against the settlement that earned it. Leaving it in trust muddies balance 3; sweeping it out without a settlement to point at is the unmatched-disbursement exception above.

What if the month is out of balance and I can't find why?

Record it as out of balance with the difference and what you checked. An honest open item, resolved next month, is defensible. A plug is not.
