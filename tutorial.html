Step by step

  1. Start your free month and save your key

    On getsaleledger.com, click Start free month. Stripe handles checkout; a card is required, but nothing is charged for 30 days, and you can cancel before then from your billing page. The page after checkout shows your license key, SL-XXXX-XXXX-XXXX-XXXX, and the download link. Copy the key somewhere safe — it's also kept with your subscription, so you can return to that page any time.

  2. Open SaleLedger and activate

    Double-click SaleLedger.exe. It opens in your web browser at 127.0.0.1:5151 — a page served by your own computer, not by us. Paste your key and click Activate.

    Windows may show a "Windows protected your PC" notice the first time because the app isn't code-signed yet. Choose More info → Run anyway.
    The key is the only thing SaleLedger ever sends anywhere, about every six hours, to confirm your subscription. Your ledger, bank statement, and consignor data never leave this computer.

  3. Gather three files for the month

    Settlement export from Auction Flex. Consignor Summary (Reports #21/#23), Totals by Consignment Order (#24/#25), or Totals by Item (#26/#27) — whichever you already run. Save it as CSV.

    QuickBooks / IIF export. In Auction Flex, Pay Consignors → Export to QuickBooks for the same auction.

    Trust account bank statement. Download from your bank as CSV, Excel, or the Quicken / QuickBooks "Web Connect" file (OFX/QFX).

    For the practice run, these are the three files in the ZIP.

  4. Fill in Option A and run

    Under Option A — Auction Flex + QuickBooks, choose the three files. Enter the auction number, the trust account's opening balance on the first day of the period, and the as-of date. For the practice set: 246, 150000.00, 2026-08-31. Click Run reconciliation.

    SaleLedger matches consignors between the settlement export and the IIF file by name. If a name is spelled differently in the two files, that consignor's payout is left out and the report names it — it never guesses.

  5. Read the result

    The report opens with one status — IN BALANCE or OUT OF BALANCE — and the three figures that have to agree: the bank's adjusted balance, the trust control balance from your records, and the consignor subledger total. In the practice month they're $166,730.00, $150,475.00, and $150,000.00, so the month is out of balance and the report says so.

  6. Work through the exceptions

    Every gap becomes a numbered exception in plain English. Practice with these:

    Disbursement mismatch — Consignor A's check cleared for $27,700.00 but the ledger recorded $27,200.00: a $500 overpayment to chase.
    Unmatched receipt — a $20,000.00 deposit with no auction or consignor behind it: find the source before it sits in trust.
    Unmatched disbursement — a $2,950.00 wire to the operating account with no authorization: the classic commingling finding.
    Bank fee — a $95.00 monthly service fee the bank took from trust: record it, or move it to operating.

    SaleLedger identifies the problem; you decide the fix. It never assumes an explanation.

  7. Keep the report and the check register

    The report ends with a check register — date, payee, check number, amount — which is what Wisconsin SPS 125.12(1) and similar state bookkeeping rules ask for. Save the page (or print to PDF) with your monthly records. Every run is also added to a local history, so you can show a state examiner each month you reconciled.

  8. Next month

    SaleLedger pre-fills the opening balance from last period's actual bank ending balance, so you don't retype it. Bring in three new files, click Run, read the result. If you're checking a full month with several auctions, include settlement data for every auction that had activity — otherwise their deposits and checks show up as unexplained.
