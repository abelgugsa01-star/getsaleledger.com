The three files behind a trust reconciliation.

Which Auction Flex report to run, how to get the QuickBooks payout file, what your bank has to give you — and the four small things that make the three refuse to line up.

1 · The settlement export (what you owe, and to whom)

This is your auction software's statement of the auction: what sold, for how much, commission, fees and the net owed to each consignor. From Auction Flex any of these works — use the one you already run:

  • Consignor Summary — Reports #21 / #23. One line per consignor: the quickest for month-end.
  • Totals by Consignment Order — #24 / #25. One line per consignment, useful when a consignor has several.
  • Totals by Item — #26 / #27. Item level; biggest file, most detail.

Save it as CSV, one file per auction. Run it after the final settlement, not before — a pre-settlement export changes when late fees or buybacks post.

2 · The QuickBooks / IIF file (what you actually paid)

In Auction Flex, Pay Consignors → Export to QuickBooks for the same auction. The IIF file carries what the settlement export does not: the check number, the payee as it went to the bank and the date the payout was made. That is what gets matched against cleared checks and what fills the check register your state board asks for.

One auction, one settlement export, one IIF. If a month had three auctions, bring three of each. An auction you leave out shows up as unexplained deposits and checks.

3 · The trust account bank statement (what the bank saw)

Download it for the full period from online banking as CSV, Excel, or the Quicken / QuickBooks "Web Connect" file (OFX/QFX). Not a PDF — a PDF has no machine-readable dates and amounts, and retyping it is where errors start. Only the trust account: the operating account's statement is not part of this.
